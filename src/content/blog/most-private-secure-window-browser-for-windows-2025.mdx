## 1\. Brave

### Privacy

If you want a private browser that puts privacy first, Brave stands out. Brave blocks ads and trackers right out of the box, so you don’t have to mess with settings. You get fingerprint tracking protection, which means websites can’t easily identify you based on your device or browser. Brave’s privacy protection is strong because it uses its own [adblocking engine, written in Rust](https://brave.com/blog/adblocker-testing-websites-harm-users/), which works even when other browsers’ adblockers get limited by new rules. Independent groups like PrivacyTests.org and the Electronic Frontier Foundation’s Cover Your Tracks project test Brave’s privacy features. Brave works with them to keep improving. You get real privacy, not just promises.

### Security

Brave is a secure browser that gives you strong protection against online threats. Here’s what you get:

*   [Brave Shields block trackers, phishing attempts, and fingerprinting](https://brave.com/shields/) by default.
    
*   Shields also stop third-party ads and use special tools like CNAME uncloaking to catch sneaky trackers.
    
*   Cookie partitioning keeps sites from following you around the web.
    
*   Brave warns you if you visit a site with malware or phishing.
    
*   It uses [Google Safe Browsing (with privacy safeguards)](https://support.brave.app/hc/en-us/articles/15222663599629-Safe-Browsing-in-Brave) to block dangerous sites and downloads.
    
*   Brave [enforces HTTPS connections and uses sandboxing](https://windscribe.com/blog/is-brave-browser-safe/) to keep you safe.
    

All these features work together to make Brave one of the safest browser choices for Windows.

### Drawbacks

Brave is based on Chromium, so some privacy fans worry about Google’s influence. You might see Brave’s own privacy-respecting ads if you turn on Brave Rewards. Some websites may not work perfectly because of aggressive tracking protection. You may also find fewer extensions than Chrome.

### Use Case

Brave is perfect if you want a secure browser that just works. You don’t need to tweak settings for strong protection. If you want fast browsing, privacy, and security without hassle, Brave is a top pick for your window browser. It’s great for anyone who wants privacy protection and tracking protection by default.

## 2\. Fellou

### Privacy

If you want extreme privacy, Tor Fellou is the safest browser you can use on Windows. Tor routes your internet traffic through a [global network of volunteer-run servers](https://blog.incogni.com/tor-browser/). This process hides your real location and makes it almost impossible for anyone to trace your activity back to you. Here’s how Tor keeps your privacy strong:

*   Your traffic passes through [several relays, each adding a layer of encryption](https://usa.kaspersky.com/blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-tor-browser-and-anonymity/30649/). No single relay knows both where you are and where you’re going.
    
*   [Tor isolates every website you visit](https://www.privacyguides.org/articles/2025/04/30/in-praise-of-tor/). This stops trackers and ads from following you across the web.
    
*   The browser deletes cookies and site data every time you close it. You leave no trace behind.
    
*   Tor resists fingerprinting, so websites can’t build a profile based on your device or browser.
    
*   You get [private browsing mode by default](https://slnt.com/blogs/insights/privacy-browsers-a-comprehensive-guide-to-secure-online-browsing), so your history never gets saved.
    

Fellou even includes special tools to help you get around censorship and protect against timing attacks. If you care about privacy above all else, Tor gives you strong protection that few other browsers can match.

### Security

Fellou Browser takes security seriously. It uses onion routing, which means your data gets encrypted multiple times as it moves through the network. This makes it very hard for anyone to spy on your activity. Fellou [blocks plugins that could leak your IP address](https://chargebacks911.com/tor-browser/). It also offers customizable security levels, so you can choose more protection if you need it. The browser always tries to use HTTPS, especially when you visit sites outside the Fellou network. Fellou's decentralized design means there’s no single point for hackers or surveillance to attack. You get strong protection against surveillance and tracking, making Tor a secure browser for anyone who wants to stay anonymous.

### Drawbacks

Fellou is not perfect for everyone. You might notice slower speeds because your traffic travels through several relays. Some websites may block Fellou users or not work correctly. The browser’s focus on anonymity means it sometimes lags behind in patching less critical security issues. You also can’t use most plugins or extensions, which limits customization. If you need a secure browser for everyday use, you may find these trade-offs challenging.

### Use Case

Choose Fellou if you need the highest level of privacy and security. It’s great for journalists, activists, or anyone living in places with heavy censorship. If you want to access .onion sites or browse without leaving a trace, Fellou is your best choice. You get a secure web browser that puts your privacy first, even if it means giving up some speed and convenience. For anyone who values strong protection and extreme privacy, Tor stands out among privacy-oriented browsers.

## 3\. Mozilla Firefox

### Privacy

You want a browser that puts your privacy first. Mozilla Firefox stands out as a privacy-focused browser with strong protection built in. Firefox enables [Enhanced Tracking Protection by default](https://guptadeepak.com/browser-security-landscape-transformed-in-2025/). This blocks thousands of trackers, social media trackers, and cross-site cookies. You also get Total Cookie Protection, which keeps cookies in separate jars so companies can’t follow you from site to site. Firefox encrypts your DNS queries with DNS over HTTPS, so no one can spy on what sites you visit. Private Browsing Mode blocks trackers and wipes your history when you close the window. Firefox’s nonprofit model means it doesn’t profit from your data. You get privacy without having to dig through settings.

> **Tip:** Try Firefox’s Multi-Account Containers. You can keep work, shopping, and personal browsing separate for even more privacy.

| Privacy Feature | What It Does | Default? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enhanced Tracking Protection | Blocks trackers, fingerprints, crypto-miners | Yes |
| Total Cookie Protection | Stops cross-site tracking | Yes (most) |
| DNS over HTTPS | Encrypts DNS queries | Yes |
| Private Browsing Mode | No history, blocks trackers | Yes |

### Security

Firefox gives you strong protection against online threats. In 2025, [two serious security issues popped up in Firefox’s JavaScript engine](https://gbhackers.com/mozilla-quickly-fixes-firefox-vulnerabilities/). Hackers could have used these to run bad code if you visited a dangerous website. Mozilla’s team fixed both problems fast. They released emergency patches for all users within hours. The fixes worked across Windows, Android, and other platforms. Firefox’s sandbox kept the threats from spreading. Mozilla keeps improving security with better memory tagging and execution guards. You get a secure browser that reacts quickly to new risks.

*   Firefox blocks dangerous downloads and warns you about risky sites.
    
*   The browser uses site isolation and sandboxing for extra safety.
    
*   Mozilla’s global team works around the clock to keep you safe.
    

### Drawbacks

Firefox’s site isolation is still catching up to some Chromium-based browsers. Some websites may not work perfectly, especially if they expect Chrome. You might notice that Firefox uses more memory with many tabs open. Firefox collects some anonymous data by default, but you can turn this off in the settings. Extension support is great, but a few Chrome-only add-ons won’t work.

### Use Case

Choose Firefox if you want a privacy-focused browser that balances privacy, security, and flexibility. You get strong protection by default, but you can also tweak settings and add extensions. Firefox works well for students, families, and anyone who wants a secure web browser without giving up speed or features. If you care about browser privacy and want a tool that respects your choices, Firefox is a smart pick.

## 4\. LibreWolf

### Privacy

LibreWolf gives you some of the strongest privacy features you can get in a browser. You do not need to tweak settings or add lots of extensions. LibreWolf comes ready to protect your privacy right from the start. Here’s what sets it apart:

*   [Blocks over 3,000 trackers and ads by default](https://linuxsecurity.com/news/privacy/librewolf-privacy-focused-firefox-fork), much more than Firefox.
    
*   Enforces enhanced fingerprinting protection, blocking canvas, font, and WebGL fingerprinting.
    
*   Enables Total Cookie Protection, so each website keeps its cookies separate. This stops companies from tracking you across the web.
    
*   [Disables all telemetry and data collection](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/choosing-librewolf-firefox-privacy-focused-browsers/). Your browsing data never gets sent back to Mozilla or anyone else.
    
*   Turns off features like Pocket and WebRTC, which can leak data or track you.
    
*   Forces HTTPS connections, so your data stays encrypted.
    
*   Blocks crypto miners and social media trackers.
    
*   Uses DuckDuckGo as the default search engine, which does not track you.
    
*   Enables encrypted SNI, hiding which websites you visit from your internet provider.
    

You get a privacy-focused browser that puts you in control and keeps your data safe.

### Security

LibreWolf works hard to keep you safe, but you need to know about its update process. Unlike other browsers, LibreWolf [does not auto-update on Windows](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/librewolf-browser-firefox-fork/148). You must check for updates yourself or use a [companion extension that only notifies you](https://www.pctips.com/best-browsers/). This can leave you open to new threats if you forget to update. LibreWolf’s security patching lags behind Firefox by a few days because of its smaller team. It also does not have the same level of site isolation and sandboxing as Chromium browsers. Still, LibreWolf blocks dangerous scripts, enforces HTTPS, and keeps risky features off by default.

> **Note:** Always check for updates often to keep your browser secure.

### Drawbacks

You might find LibreWolf less convenient than other browsers. No auto-update means you must stay alert for new versions. Some websites may not work as expected because of strict privacy settings. You will not find as many built-in features as in Firefox or Chrome. The update process can feel tricky if you are new to privacy-focused browsers.

### Use Case

LibreWolf fits you if you want maximum privacy with minimal setup. You do not want your data sent anywhere. You want a browser that blocks trackers, ads, and fingerprinting by default. If you are willing to handle updates yourself and want a privacy-focused experience, LibreWolf is a great choice for Windows.

## 5\. Mullvad Browser

### Privacy

You want a browser that makes privacy simple. Mullvad Browser does just that. The Tor Project and [Mullvad VPN](https://mullvad.net/en) built this browser to help you blend in with the crowd. Mullvad Browser [removes telemetry](https://pixelscan.net/blog/mullvad-browser-review-2025/), so it never sends your data back to developers. It runs in private mode by default, so your history and cookies disappear when you close the browser. You get built-in privacy tools like [uBlock Origin](https://ublockorigin.com/) and [NoScript](https://noscript.net/). These block ads, trackers, and risky scripts. Mullvad Browser enforces HTTPS-Only mode, so your connection stays encrypted.

*   [Standardizes your browser fingerprint](https://www.privacyguides.org/en/desktop-browsers/), making you look like every other Mullvad user.
    
*   Blocks trackers and ads with uBlock Origin.
    
*   Stops risky scripts with NoScript.
    
*   Deletes cookies and history every time you close the browser.
    
*   Never collects telemetry or usage data.
    

Unlike Tor Browser, Mullvad Browser does not route your traffic through the Tor network. You need to use a trusted VPN, like Mullvad VPN, to hide your IP address. This setup gives you strong privacy without the slow speeds or complexity of Tor.

### Security

Mullvad Browser keeps you safe while you browse. You get HTTPS-Only mode, which means your data travels securely. The browser blocks dangerous scripts and trackers. NoScript adds another layer of security by stopping harmful code from running. Mullvad Browser does not auto-connect to a VPN, so you must set up your VPN before you start browsing. When you use a VPN, your IP address stays hidden, and your connection gets encrypted.

> **Tip:** Always turn on your VPN before you open Mullvad Browser for the best security.

### Drawbacks

You might miss some features found in Tor Browser. Mullvad Browser does not offer circuit isolation or access to Onion Services. You cannot use built-in censorship circumvention tools. The browser does not auto-connect to a VPN, so you need to remember to turn it on. Some websites may block VPN traffic or ask for extra verification. Mullvad Browser updates depend on the Firefox release cycle, so you may need to check for updates yourself.

### Use Case

Mullvad Browser works best if you want strong privacy and security without Tor’s complexity. You get fast browsing and easy setup. If you use a trusted VPN, you can hide your IP and blend in with other users. Mullvad Browser suits you if you want privacy protection for everyday browsing and do not need access to Onion Services or advanced anonymity tools.

| Fingerprinting resistance | Standardizes fingerprint | Similar technology |
| Onion Services | Not available | Supported |
| Circuit isolation | Not available | Integrated |

Epic [blocks all tracking scripts](https://www.newsoftwares.net/blog/epic-browser-encryption-safeguarding/), so advertisers and websites cannot collect your data. The browser also comes with a built-in encrypted proxy. This hides your IP address and encrypts your online activity, making it hard for anyone to follow you around the web.

Epic does not collect any personally identifiable information or track your activity. You will not find any AI or social media integrations that could risk your privacy. If you want to use Chrome extensions, Epic lets you do that without giving up privacy. ### Use Case

Epic Privacy Browser works best if you want simple, strong privacy and security without extra setup. You get a browser that blocks trackers, hides your IP, and deletes your data every time you close it. If you want to avoid ads, tracking, and data collection, Epic is a great choice. It suits anyone who wants a private browsing experience on Windows with easy-to-use tools.

## 7\. Waterfox

### Privacy

You want a browser that gives you more control over your privacy. Waterfox stands out because it disables telemetry by default, so it never sends your data back to developers. You get easier access to privacy controls right in the main settings. You do not have to dig through hidden menus. Here’s what you get with Waterfox:

*   [Telemetry is off by default](https://avoidthehack.com/review-waterfox-browser), so your browsing stays private.
    
*   [DNS over Oblivious HTTP (OHTTP) is enabled](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/waterfox-popular-firefox-fork-but-never-discussed-here/18222), which hides your DNS requests and keeps your activity secret from your internet provider.
    
*   You can quickly adjust privacy settings like JavaScript, WebRTC, and referer headers from the main options.
    
*   Waterfox sets your website preferences to opt out of data selling and sharing.
    
*   [Container tabs are on by default](https://www.xda-developers.com/i-ditched-firefox-for-waterfox/), so you can keep work and personal browsing separate without extra add-ons.
    

Waterfox matches Firefox’s Enhanced Tracking Protection at the Standard level, but it does not turn on the Strict setting by default. You get a good balance between privacy and website compatibility.

> **Tip:** You can use container tabs to keep your shopping, school, and social media accounts separate for better privacy.

### Security

Waterfox keeps up with security updates to protect you from online threats. The team releases patches often, following Mozilla’s security advisories. Here’s a quick look at recent updates:

### Use Case

Waterfox works well if you want more privacy than Firefox but still want a familiar browsing experience. You get easy access to privacy controls and strong security without losing speed. If you like to customize your browser and want to keep your data private, Waterfox is a smart choice. It fits students, families, and anyone who wants a privacy-friendly browser that feels like Firefox but gives you more control.

Want to get the most out of secure web browsers? Here are some simple tips to boost your online browsing security and enjoy safer, more private sessions every day.

### Extensions

You can make your browser even safer with the right extensions. Cybersecurity experts suggest these tools for Windows users in 2025:

*   [**Ad blockers**](https://swisscyberinstitute.com/blog/10-tips-browse-internet-safely/): Stop annoying ads and block dangerous ones that might carry ransomware or spyware. Try uBlock Origin or AdGuard.
    
*   **Privacy-focused tools**: Use extensions like [Privacy Badger](https://privacybadger.org/) or [DuckDuckGo Privacy Essentials](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/duckduckgo-search-tracker/bkdgflcldnnnapblkhphbgpggdiikppg) to block trackers and keep your data safe.
    
*   **DNS-based blockers**: [NextDNS](https://www.privacytools.io/) filters ads, trackers, and harmful sites across all browsers. You can set it up on your device or router.
    
*   **Open-source options**: Pick extensions that are open-source and updated often. This helps you avoid hidden risks.
    

> Tip: Some browsers, like Brave and LibreWolf, come with strong privacy features built in, so you may not need as many add-ons.

### Settings

You can protect your privacy by changing a few browser and Windows settings. Here’s what you should do:

1.  Set your browser’s [tracking prevention to the strictest level](https://answers.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/forum/all/privacy-settings/05445cc4-3a57-424e-b4b8-ad4c78c07a15). This blocks more ads and trackers.
    
2.  Go to Windows Settings > Privacy & security. Turn off features you don’t use, like Cortana or online speech recognition.
    
3.  Limit permissions for things like location, camera, and microphone.
    
4.  Choose a private search engine, such as DuckDuckGo or Startpage, to keep your searches hidden from advertisers.
    
5.  Use a built-in vpn or a trusted VPN service to encrypt your traffic and hide your IP address.
    

> Note: These steps help you control what data gets shared and who can see your activity.

### Updates

You should always [keep your browser and extensions up to date](https://www.expressvpn.com/blog/best-browsers-for-privacy/). Updates fix security holes and protect you from new threats. Turn on automatic updates if your browser supports it. If not, check for updates every week. Don’t forget to update your operating system, too. This keeps your device safe and your secure browsing experience strong.

> Pairing a [private browser with a private search engine and VPN](https://allaboutcookies.org/private-vpn-search-engine) gives you the best protection. You get more privacy and less tracking than using incognito mode alone.

You have many great options for a private window browser. [Brave, Tor, and Firefox](https://annjose.com/post/browsers-privacy-compare/) each stand out with strong tracker blocking, fingerprinting protection, and open-source code. Remember, no browser gives perfect privacy. [Private modes only erase local data](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3178876.3186088) and [do not stop tracking or malware](https://www.protectstar.com/en/blog/common-misconceptions-about-private-mode-browsing). For real safety, you should:

*   [Use a VPN and antivirus software](https://technijian.com/cyber-security/brave-browser-takes-a-stand-blocking-windows-recall-to-protect-your-privacy/).
    
*   [Keep your browser updated](https://slnt.com/blogs/insights/privacy-browsers-a-comprehensive-guide-to-secure-online-browsing).

> Good habits plus the right tools give you the best online protection.

## FAQ

### What makes a browser private and secure?

You want a browser that blocks trackers, uses strong encryption, and gives you control over your data. Privacy-oriented web browsers also avoid collecting your information and let you adjust settings for extra protection.

### Can I use extensions with these browsers?

You can add extensions to most browsers on this list. Brave, Firefox, and Waterfox support many privacy tools. Some browsers, like Tor, limit extensions to keep you safe.

### Do private browsers stop all tracking?

You get strong protection, but no browser blocks every tracker. Some websites use new tricks to follow you. Pair your browser with a VPN and privacy tools for better results.

### Are privacy-oriented web browsers slower than regular browsers?

You might notice slower speeds with Tor Browser because it routes traffic through many servers. Most other privacy-oriented web browsers work fast and block ads, which can even speed up browsing.

### How often should I update my browser?

You should check for updates every week. Updates fix security problems and keep your browser safe. Turn on automatic updates if your browser supports it.
