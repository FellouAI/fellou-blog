### Citation Analysis

Citation analysis lets you see how research papers are linked. With an ai browser, you can study papers and see which ones cite others. This is important for academic research and systematic reviews. The ai browser uses smart methods to show these links.

You will see that ai browsers use [citation network methods](https://thebibliomagician.wordpress.com/2020/07/23/ai-based-citation-evaluation-tools-good-bad-or-ugly/). These include co-citation, where two papers are listed together in a reference list, and bibliographic coupling, where two papers cite the same source. These ways help you find related works and spot big trends in the literature.

Modern ai tools go further by using natural language processing and machine learning. They can sort citations as supporting, disputing, or influential. This gives you a better look at how papers connect. You can see which studies agree or disagree with each other. This is helpful for qualitative analysis and for seeing the impact of research.

When you use citation analysis, remember there are some problems. Bias in scholarly writing can change the results. For example, papers with lots of citations may get more notice, even if they are not the best. Language and culture differences in writing can also make things harder. Ai cannot always tell why a citation was made, so you should use your own thinking when looking at the results.

> Callout: Use citation analysis to find important papers for your systematic review. This helps you build a strong base for your research.

Citation analysis with ai browsers gives you new ways to look at academic papers. You can map out networks, find key studies, and see gaps in the research. This makes it easier to study research data and helps you make better choices in your own projects.

## Common Pitfalls

### Limitations

When you use an AI browser for research, you may face some limits. AI browsers work best with clear and well-structured articles. If a paper has a complex layout or uses images for text, the AI might miss some details. Some websites use strong security tools that block AI agents. You may not get all the data you want from these sites.

AI browsers can struggle with very large files or long documents. If you upload too many papers at once, the system may slow down. Sometimes, the AI may not understand special terms or rare formats. You should always check the results to make sure the data is correct.

> Note: Always review your extracted data. This helps you catch missing or incorrect information early.

### Troubleshooting

If you run into problems during [data extraction](https://www.algolia.com/doc/tools/crawler/troubleshooting/extraction-issues/), you can follow these steps to fix them:

1.  Make sure you have access to the website. Add the domain to your allowlist and check that the AI browser’s user agent is not blocked by site security or robots.txt.
    
2.  Look at any pages that did not load. Some pages may have special blocks or errors.
    
3.  Use your browser’s developer tools to check the CSS selectors. Update them if the AI is not picking up the right data.
    
4.  Turn on JavaScript rendering if the page uses dynamic content. This helps the AI see all the information.
    
5.  Check if canonical URLs are causing pages to be skipped. Adjust your settings to include all needed pages.
    
6.  Watch out for data limits. Make sure each item has a unique ID and avoid circular links or very long IDs.
    
7.  If you see timeouts or errors, try reducing the page size or the number of links. Exclude any pages that cause problems.
    
8.  Test how the website responds to different user agents. Change the settings if the AI browser is not getting the right content.
    

> Tip: Try these steps one at a time. You will often solve most extraction issues by checking access, selectors, and site settings.
