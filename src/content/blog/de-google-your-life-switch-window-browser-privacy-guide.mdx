You want more control over your digital life. You notice how often your window browser connects you to the web, but did you know Google Chrome is the main choice for [almost two-thirds of internet users worldwide](https://www.aboutchromebooks.com/global-chrome-user-base/)? That means your habits and personal data often end up in Google’s hands. Many people do not realize Chrome [records what you type, tracks your visits, and collects details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy_concerns_with_Google) unless you change settings yourself. You do not need to be a tech expert to take back your privacy. Just a few simple changes can give you more security and control.

## Key Takeaways

*   Check your browser for Google connections by reviewing settings, extensions, and data syncing to understand how much information Google collects.
    
*   Switch to a privacy-focused browser like Brave, Firefox, or Tor to reduce tracking and gain stronger control over your online data.
    
*   Adjust your browser’s privacy settings by disabling third-party cookies, enabling HTTPS-only mode, and blocking trackers to protect your information.
    
*   Use trusted privacy extensions such as uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger, and HTTPS Everywhere to block ads, trackers, and secure your connections.
    
*   Replace Google search and apps with private alternatives and regularly review your privacy settings to keep your digital life safe and under your control.
    

## Check Your Window Browser

Before you can de-Google your life, you need to know what you’re working with. Let’s start by checking your window browser for any Google connections.

### Find Google Services

You might wonder, “Am I using a Google-linked browser?” [Most people know if they’re using Chrome](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/9317754/is-it-possible-to-check-if-google-chrome-is-installed-on-a-clients-computer), but sometimes it’s easy to forget. Here’s how you can tell:

*   Look at the icon on your desktop or taskbar. Chrome has a colorful circle with red, green, yellow, and blue.
    
*   When you open your window browser, check the top left or right for the browser name. Chrome will say “Google Chrome.”
    
*   If you see options like “Sign in to Chrome” or “You and Google” in the settings, you’re likely using a Google-linked browser.
    

> **Tip:** If you use browsers like Microsoft Edge or Firefox, you’re not directly using Google Chrome, but you might still have Google services or extensions installed.

### Review Data and Settings

Now, let’s dig into your browser’s data and settings. This helps you spot where Google might be collecting your info.

1.  Open your window browser and go to the settings menu (usually three dots or lines in the corner).
    
2.  Look for sections like “You and Google” or “Profiles.” Here, you can see if you’re signed in with a Google account.
    
3.  [Check if sync is turned on](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/8887770/google-chrome-sync-check-if-enabled-via-api-extension). Syncing means your bookmarks, passwords, and history are sent to Google’s servers. You can visit the sync settings page to review what’s being shared.
    
4.  Review your extensions. [Some extensions connect to Google and can track your activity](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/browser-extensions-put-millions-google-chrome-users-risk). [Dangerous extensions can steal your info](https://blog.smu.edu/itconnect/2024/06/27/the-hidden-dangers-of-web-browser-extensions-what-you-need-to-know/), track your browsing, or even hijack your system.  
    
5.  Take a look at your browsing history, saved passwords, and autofill data. Here’s a quick table to help you find them:
    

| Data Type | Where to Find It in Chrome Settings | What It Includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browsing History | History > History | List of sites you’ve visited |
| Saved Passwords | Settings > Autofill > Passwords | Usernames and passwords |
| Cookies | Settings > Privacy and Security > Cookies | Site data and tracking info |
| Form Autofill Data | Settings > Autofill > Addresses and more | Names, addresses, phone numbers |

> **Note:** Extensions with broad permissions can see almost everything you do online. Remove any you don’t trust or use.

By checking these areas, you’ll know exactly how much of your digital life is tied to Google through your window browser.

### Install and Import Data

Switching to a new window browser is easier than you think. You can keep your bookmarks and passwords, so you don’t lose anything important. Here’s how you do it:

1.  [**Export your bookmarks and passwords from Chrome**](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4230155/how-to-move-bookmarks-between-windows-account-incl)**:**
    
    *   Open Chrome and go to Settings.
        
    *   Find Passwords under Autofill.
        
    *   Click the three dots next to Saved Passwords and select Export Passwords.
        
    *   Enter your Windows username and password to confirm.
        
    *   Save the .csv file somewhere safe.
        
2.  **Install your new browser:**
    
    *   Visit the official website for Brave, Firefox, or another browser you like.
        
    *   Download the installer and run it.
        
    *   Follow the prompts to finish installation.
        
3.  **Import your data:**
    
    *   Open your new browser and look for Import or Import Data in the settings.
        
    *   Choose the .csv file you saved for passwords.
        
    *   Import bookmarks by selecting the Chrome bookmarks file.
        
    *   Some browsers let you import everything at once. Others may ask you to do bookmarks and passwords separately.
        

> **Note:** Make sure Chrome is closed before you export or import data. If Chrome is open, the database might be locked and you could lose information.

Here’s a quick checklist to avoid common mistakes:

*   Double-check that you exported all your bookmarks and passwords.
    
*   If you use scripts to export data, make sure [filenames have quotation marks](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/10782050/google-chrome-errors-while-exporting-xls-file-using-php) if they contain spaces or commas.
    
*   Always keep a backup copy of your exported files.
    

Privacy-focused browsers [collect much less data than Chrome](https://cyberpress.org/the-worst-browser-for-data-collection/). For example, Brave only collects basic identifiers and usage data. Tor doesn’t collect any user data at all. Firefox collects some contact details and diagnostics, but you can adjust these settings for more privacy.

When you choose a new browser, look for features like:

*   [Anti-fingerprinting](https://www.privacyguides.org/en/desktop-browsers/) technology
    
*   Private browsing modes
    
*   DNS over HTTPS (DoH)
    
*   End-to-end encrypted sync
    
*   Built-in content blockers
    

Brave and Firefox offer these features out of the box. Mullvad Browser is another strong choice if you want advanced anti-fingerprinting without using Tor.

Switching your window browser is a big step toward controlling your data. You’ll notice fewer ads, less tracking, and more peace of mind every time you go online.

## Set Up Privacy

You’ve picked a privacy-focused browser. Now, let’s make sure you get the most protection possible. Setting up privacy isn’t hard, and you can do it in just a few steps.

### Adjust Settings

Start by tweaking your browser’s privacy settings. These changes help block trackers, keep your data safe, and stop unwanted ads from following you around.

**Here’s what you should do:**

1.  [**Disable third-party cookies**](https://www.expressvpn.com/blog/best-browsers-for-privacy/)**.** This stops websites from tracking you across the internet. You’ll see fewer creepy ads that seem to know what you just searched for.
    
2.  **Turn on HTTPS-only mode.** This makes sure every site you visit uses encryption. Your information stays safe from snoops.
    
3.  **Customize tracker-blocking features.** Brave has Shields, Firefox offers [Enhanced Tracking Protection](https://adguard.com/en/blog/browsers-privacy-top-protection-guide.html), and Vivaldi lets you block trackers and ads. Set these to the strictest level for the best privacy.
    
4.  **Enable “Do Not Track” and “Global Privacy Control.”** These signals tell websites not to track or sell your data.
    
5.  **Adjust permissions for camera, microphone, and location.** Set these to “Ask” or “Block.” You decide when a site can use them.
    
6.  **Use Private Browsing mode.** This keeps your browsing history and cookies from being saved on your computer.
    
7.  **Disable telemetry.** This stops your browser from sending usage data back to the company.
    
8.  **Turn on DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH).** This encrypts your web requests, making it harder for anyone to see what sites you visit.
    
9.  **Update your browser often.** Updates fix security holes and keep you protected.
    

> **Tip:** [Brave blocks fingerprinting](https://www.howtogeek.com/sick-of-being-tracked-these-browsers-put-your-privacy-first/) and Google AMP pages by default. Firefox lets you set Enhanced Tracking Protection to “Strict.” Vivaldi needs you to manually turn on tracker and ad blocking.

Here’s a quick table to help you find these settings in popular browsers:

| Browser | Key Privacy Settings Location | What to Enable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brave | Settings > Shields | Block fingerprinting, trackers, ads |
| Firefox | Settings > Privacy & Security | Enhanced Tracking Protection, DoH |
| Vivaldi | Settings > Privacy | Block trackers/ads, third-party cookies |

You don’t need to change every setting at once. Start with the basics, then explore more options as you get comfortable.

### Add Privacy Extensions

Privacy extensions give you extra protection. They block ads, stop trackers, and make sure your connection stays secure.

**Here are the top picks:**

*   [**uBlock Origin**](https://blog.devolutions.net/2020/12/8-browser-extensions-to-protect-your-privacy/)**:** Blocks ads and trackers. It’s fast and uses very little memory. You’ll notice fewer pop-ups and less clutter.
    
*   **Privacy Badger:** Learns which trackers follow you and blocks them automatically. It also sends signals to websites asking them not to track you.
    
*   [**HTTPS Everywhere**](https://www.h2kinfosys.com/blog/top-10-chrome-extensions-that-prevent-you-from-getting-hacked/)**:** Switches sites to secure connections whenever possible. This keeps your data safe from hackers and snoops.
    

> **Note:** uBlock Origin can block thousands of ads and trackers. Privacy Badger removes tracking from links on big sites like Google and Facebook. HTTPS Everywhere protects you from surveillance and account hijacking.

**How to add extensions:**

1.  Open your browser’s extension or add-on store.
    
2.  Search for “uBlock Origin,” “Privacy Badger,” and “HTTPS Everywhere.”
    
3.  Click “Add” or “Install” for each extension.
    
4.  Check the extension settings. Most work right away, but you can tweak them for even more privacy.
    

**Here’s a quick checklist:**

*   ✅ uBlock Origin installed and active
    
*   ✅ Privacy Badger learning and blocking trackers
    
*   ✅ HTTPS Everywhere switching sites to secure mode
    

> **Tip:** You don’t need every privacy extension out there. These three cover the basics and work well together.

### Disable Personalized Ads and Clear Google History

Even after switching browsers, Google might still show you personalized ads or keep your search history. You can stop this with a few quick steps.

**To turn off personalized ads:**

1.  Go to your Google Account’s Ad Settings.
    
2.  Find the option for ad personalization.
    
3.  Toggle it off. This stops Google from using your data to target ads.
    

**To clear your Google search history:**

1.  Open your Google Account and go to Search History.
    
2.  Delete specific searches or clear everything.
    
3.  Set up automatic deletion so old activity gets wiped regularly.
    

**Extra steps for privacy:**

*   Use privacy-focused browsers like Brave or Firefox. They block most trackers and ads by default.
    
*   Review app and service permissions linked to your Google Account. Remove anything you don’t use.
    
*   Try Incognito Mode for browsing without saving history or cookies. Remember, this doesn’t hide your activity from websites or your internet provider.
    

> **Tip:** Disabling ad personalization and clearing your history means Google knows less about you. You’ll see fewer targeted ads and keep your searches private.

[**Quick steps for Chrome users**](https://www.makeuseof.com/stop-chrome-history-targeted-ads/)**:**

```
1. Open Chrome and click the three dots in the top-right corner.
2. Go to Settings > Privacy and security.
3. Click Ad privacy or type chrome://settings/adPrivacy in the address bar.
4. Turn off Ad topics, Site-suggested ads, and Ad measured.
5. Customize subcategories to block specific ad interests.

```

You’re now in control of your privacy. With strict settings, smart extensions, and less data in Google’s hands, you can browse with confidence. 🚀

## Change Search and Services

You can start your privacy journey by changing your default search engine. Google tracks your searches and builds a profile about you. Private search engines work differently. They do not collect your personal data or store your search history.

*   DuckDuckGo [does not track your searches or browsing history](https://duckduckgo.com/duckduckgo-help-pages/company/how-does-duckduckgo-protect-privacy/).
    
*   It blocks trackers from other companies.
    
*   DuckDuckGo [does not store your IP address or create a search profile](https://duckduckgo.com/privacy).
    
*   Search queries are saved only temporarily and anonymously to spot trends, never linked to you.
    
*   The company makes money from privacy-friendly ads, not by selling your data.
    
*   DuckDuckGo uses encrypted connections and prevents websites from seeing your search terms.
    
*   You can also try Startpage or Qwant. Startpage gives you Google results but removes all tracking. Qwant is based in Europe and focuses on privacy.
    

> **Tip:** DuckDuckGo offers [Global Privacy Control](https://www.mysteriumvpn.com/blog/is-duckduckgo-safe), which tells websites not to track or sell your data.

## Maintain Browser Privacy

### Regular Reviews

Keeping your browser private is not a one-time job. You need to check your settings and habits often. Start by making a habit of reviewing your privacy settings every month. This helps you spot changes and keep your data safe.

Here’s a simple checklist you can follow:

1.  [Open Windows Settings and go to Privacy](https://cyberinsider.com/windows-privacy/).
    
2.  Set Diagnostic data to Basic under Diagnostics & feedback.
    
3.  Turn off sending inking and typing data.
    
4.  Disable Delivery Optimization so your computer does not download updates from other PCs.
    
5.  In Activity history, turn off storing and sending activity history to Microsoft.
    
6.  Clear your existing activity history.
    
7.  Review app permissions. Limit access to your location, contacts, webcam, and other sensitive data.
    

You should also:

*   [Use strong, unique passwords](https://github.com/StellarSand/privacy-settings). A password manager can help you remember them.
    
*   Enable multi-factor authentication for extra security.
    
*   Keep your browser and apps updated.
    
*   Remove browser add-ons you do not use.
    
*   [Clear your cache, cookies, and history regularly](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/cyberpedia/safe-web-browsing).
    
*   [Block cross-site cookies and turn off autofill features](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/manufacturing-innovation-blog/maintaining-your-online-privacy).
    

> **Tip:** Make small changes often. This makes it harder for hackers to target you.

## FAQ

### How do I know if my browser is sharing data with Google?

Check your browser settings for Google account logins or sync options. Look for Chrome branding or Google extensions. If you see “You and Google,” your browser shares data with Google.

### Can I keep my bookmarks and passwords when I switch browsers?

Yes! Export your bookmarks and passwords from Chrome. Import them into your new browser using its settings menu. Most privacy browsers make this process simple.

### What is the safest browser for privacy?

Brave and Firefox offer strong privacy features. Tor Browser gives you the highest anonymity, but it can be slow. Choose the one that fits your needs best.

### Do privacy extensions slow down my browser?

Most privacy extensions like uBlock Origin and Privacy Badger run efficiently. You might notice faster browsing because they block ads and trackers.

### Will switching browsers stop all tracking?

Switching browsers reduces tracking, but some sites still collect data. Use privacy settings, extensions, and a VPN for extra protection. Stay alert and review your settings often.
