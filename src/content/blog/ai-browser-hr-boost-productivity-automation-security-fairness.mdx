## Addressing Concerns

### Bias and Fairness

You want your hr process to be fair for everyone. Bias can sneak into technology in many ways. It can come from the data used to train the system, the way the code works, or even how you use the tool. Sometimes, bias shows up as favoring people who look or act like you, or by making decisions based on things that do not matter for the job.

To fight bias, you can:

*   [Use training data that shows many types of people and skills](https://siliconvalley.center/blog/unmasking-bias-in-ai-algorithms).
    
*   [Set up clear rules for interviews and use the same questions for each candidate](https://www.shrm.org/labs/resources/eliminating-biases-in-hiring--structured-interviewing-and-ai-solutions).
    
*   Let the system hide names and photos when reviewing resumes.
    
*   [Check the results often and make changes if you see unfair patterns](https://www.datasciencecentral.com/ethics-driven-model-auditing-and-bias-mitigation/).
    
*   Build a team with different backgrounds to help spot hidden bias.
    

When you use these steps, you help everyone get a fair chance. You also build trust in your hr tools.

### Data Privacy and Security

You handle private information every day. Keeping it safe is your top job. Risks include leaks, hacking, and even mistakes by the system. Some companies have lost data because of weak security or attacks. [Laws like GDPR and CCPA tell you how to protect personal data](https://blog.qualys.com/product-tech/2025/02/07/ai-and-data-privacy-mitigating-risks-in-the-age-of-generative-ai-tools) and what you can and cannot do.

You can protect your team by:

*   Using tools that keep data private, like Fellou’s privacy mode.
    
*   Limiting who can see or change sensitive information.

*   Checking your system for problems often.
    
*   Training your team to spot risks and follow safe practices.
    

> Tip: Always pick hr tools that let you control privacy settings and show you how your data is used.
