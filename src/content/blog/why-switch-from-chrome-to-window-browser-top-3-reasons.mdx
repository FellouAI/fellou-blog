## Privacy and Security

### Tracking Protection

You want your online activity to stay private. Window browsers like Edge give you stronger tracking protection than Chrome. You get [three levels of tracking prevention](https://www.atlas.org/blog/web-browsers/microsoft-edge-vs-chrome-comparison): basic, balanced, and strict. This lets you control how much data you share. [Chrome collects more data by default](https://www.intrust-it.com/chrome-vs-edge-which-one-is-best/), which can worry privacy-focused users. Edge [blocks third-party trackers](https://www.okbar.org/cm_articles/why-microsoft-edge-is-becoming-a-better-choice-than-chrome-for-security-conscious-lawyers/) from collecting your browsing data. You can even turn off saving payment methods, lowering the risk of credit card theft.

*   Edge offers built-in tracking prevention that blocks third-party trackers.
    
*   You choose your level of protection: basic, balanced, or strict.
    
*   Edge gives you more control over your privacy settings.
    
*   Chrome relies on customizable privacy settings but does not enforce strict tracking prevention by default.
    

Recent tests show Edge’s tracking prevention works better than Chrome’s. You get more robust privacy features and stronger malware protection. If you care about keeping your data safe, Edge stands out.

> Tip: If you want to keep advertisers and data collectors away, try switching to a window browser with strict tracking prevention.

### Safer Browsing

You deserve a browser that keeps you safe from online threats. Edge leads in phishing and malware protection. The table below shows how Edge compares to Chrome:

| Browser | Phishing Protection Rate | Malware Protection Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft Edge | 92.3% | 97.4% |
| Google Chrome | 84.6% | 86.3% |

Edge uses advanced anti-phishing technology and [hardware isolation](https://www.cccp.com/post/a-comparison-of-the-cyber-security-built-into-our-default-browsers/). This means your device gets extra protection against attacks. Chrome offers strong security, but Edge’s reputation system and hardware-level security make browsing safer.

Edge also adds new security features often. You get warnings for insecure downloads and better control over sensitive data sharing. These updates help you avoid risks and keep your information secure.

Why settle for less? Choose a window browser that puts your safety first.

## Window Browser Performance

### Memory Usage

You want your browser to run smoothly, even when you open many tabs. Memory usage matters because it affects how fast your computer feels. Window browsers like Edge use less memory than Chrome when you open several tabs. This means you can browse, stream, and multitask without slowing down your device.

| Browser | Memory Usage (MB) | Tabs Open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edge | ~790 | 10 |
| Chrome | ~1,400 | 6 |

Edge uses less RAM than Chrome during heavy browsing. You get better performance, especially if your computer has limited memory. When you open just one tab, Edge and Chrome use similar amounts of memory. Over time, Edge manages resources more efficiently, so you avoid crashes and lag.

> Tip: If your device feels slow or overheats, switching to a window browser can help free up memory and keep things running smoothly.

### Speed and Responsiveness

You deserve a browser that responds quickly to your clicks and loads pages fast. Window browsers like Edge focus on speed and stability. Edge uses a [multi-process system](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2386841/why-might-ms-edge-be-running-more-processes-than-a), so each tab runs separately. This keeps your browsing stable and prevents crashes, even if one tab has a problem.

*   [Edge preloads pages and manages background tabs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2403334/is-edge-browser-anytime-soon-going-to-be-functiona) for faster switching.
    
*   You get smooth scrolling and quick access to downloads, history, and settings.
    
*   Edge releases memory when you close tabs, keeping your device responsive.
    

On laptops, [Edge helps your battery last longer](https://siliconangle.com/2016/06/21/using-microsofts-edge-browser-can-save-you-hours-of-laptop-battery-life/). Tests show that Edge uses less power than Chrome, so you can stream videos and read online for hours without plugging in. Real-world data from millions of devices confirms that Edge saves energy and improves battery life.

> Note: If you use an older laptop or want longer battery life, a window browser gives you more time to work and play.

Performance improvements in window browsers lead to higher user satisfaction. You get faster page loads, smoother navigation, and a better overall experience. That’s why more people choose window browsers for everyday use.

## Productivity Features

### Tab Management

You want to keep your tabs organized and easy to find. Window browsers like Edge give you unique tab management tools that Chrome does not offer. You can use [vertical tabs, which stack your open pages in a list on the side](https://www.slashgear.com/1608551/microsoft-edge-features-not-available-google-chrome/). This layout makes it simple to see tab titles and group related tabs. Edge lets you collapse the vertical tab bar to save space, and it expands when you hover over it. You can switch back to the traditional horizontal layout if you prefer. Edge also puts unused tabs to sleep, saving memory and keeping your computer fast. Chrome does not have these built-in features.

*   Vertical tabs for better organization
    
*   Automatic tab sleeping to save resources
    
*   Collapsible tab bar for more screen space
    

> Tip: Try vertical tabs if you often work with many open pages. You will find your workflow smoother and less cluttered.

### Organization Tools

You need tools that help you collect, organize, and manage your online research. Edge includes [Collections, a feature that lets you gather web pages, images, and notes](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1644988/chrome-vs-edge-best-browser-for-business.html) in one place. You can drag and drop content, add formatted notes, and organize everything by topic. Collections boost your productivity, especially for school projects or work research. Edge also integrates with Microsoft 365 and Bing Copilot AI, giving you quick access to documents, summaries, and enterprise search. Sleeping Tabs reduce memory use, so you can keep many resources open without slowing down.

Chrome offers tab groups and a rich extension library, but it lacks built-in tools like Collections and AI integration. You spend less time searching for information and more time getting things done with a window browser.

### Customization

You want your browser to fit your workflow. Edge supports a growing library of extensions, including popular tools like Grammarly and native features such as sidebar search. The browser uses Windows Defender SmartScreen and sandboxing for security, so you can customize your experience without worrying about threats. Edge integrates with Microsoft services, making it easy to sync files, edit documents, and join Teams meetings. Chrome has a larger extension ecosystem, but [Edge focuses on privacy and security](https://wedoit.co/microsoft-edge-vs-google-chrome-whats-the-difference/), giving you peace of mind.

| Aspect | Microsoft Edge | Google Chrome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extension Variety | Growing library; includes popular and native productivity extensions | Largest ecosystem; vast customization options |
| Security Features | Windows Defender SmartScreen, sandboxing, Tracking Prevention, Application Guard | Safe Browsing, Site Isolation, privacy controls, Sync |
| Ecosystem Focus | Strong Windows and Microsoft integration | Deep Google ecosystem integration |

You get more built-in productivity features and better resource management with a window browser. These tools help you multitask, organize your work, and stay secure, making your daily browsing easier and more efficient.

You want a browser that protects your privacy, runs faster, and helps you stay organized. Many users switch to a window browser because it offers [better Microsoft 365 integration](https://report.woodard.com/articles/edge-vs.-chrome-for-microsoft-365-users-p8mswr), smarter tab management, and flexible search options. You can organize your research with Collections, group tabs to reduce clutter, and use both Microsoft and Chrome extensions. If you value security, speed, and productivity, a window browser gives you more control and a smoother experience. Your choice depends on your needs, so try what works best for you.
